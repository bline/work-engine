# Candidate Expert-Practice Corpora for an Evidence-Sensitive Independent-Review Register

**Status:** Candidate-package assessment; no final papers selected  
**Prepared:** 2026-08-25  
**Intended use:** Source selection for the linguistic-register-transfer pilot

## Executive finding

Six practitioner-centered packages meet the provisional feasibility threshold: each has a defensible claim to expert standing, at least six publicly and legally readable full-text works, and material in more than one genre. They differ sharply, however, in what a derived register would be likely to activate and how cleanly surface realization can be separated from domain-specific reasoning instructions.

The ranking below is therefore a ranking of **pilot suitability for an evidence-sensitive independent-review agent**, not a ranking of scholarly merit.

1. **Nancy Leveson — system-safety review and causal boundary analysis.** Best overall fit to independent review; unusually coherent, varied, and centrally hosted corpus. The principal risk is very high semantic contamination: much of what sounds characteristic is also substantive STAMP/STPA reasoning.
2. **Andrew Gelman — model criticism and iterative evidence checking.** Best mixed-genre and stylistically varied corpus; especially useful for separating formal expert prose from informal working judgment. Risks include authorial personality, humor, topical politics, and explicit methodological advice.
3. **Mary Shaw — architectural comparison and engineering judgment.** Closest fit if the target role reviews software architecture or technical designs. The corpus is accessible and often author-led, but licensing is less uniform and the stylistic signal may be subtler.
4. **Judea Pearl — causal assumptions and counterexample-driven critique.** Exceptionally strong authorship confidence and corpus access. The formal causal vocabulary and polemical posture make semantic isolation difficult.
5. **John P. A. Ioannidis — evidence reliability and research-system critique.** The cleanest open-access package and an excellent match for bias, replication, and evidentiary reliability. Coauthorship and a potentially over-contrarian claim posture are the main concerns.
6. **Deborah G. Mayo — severe testing and error-probing argument.** Highly distinctive and strongly authored across papers, exchanges, and blog responses. The central discourse pattern is so tightly bound to the semantics of “severity” that a realization-only profile may retain little of what makes the register useful.

**Shortlist for a first extraction-feasibility comparison:** Leveson, Gelman, and Shaw. These three span safety review, statistical model criticism, and engineering design review while offering meaningfully different register hypotheses. This is not a final corpus selection.

## Evaluation method

Candidates were screened against six criteria:

| Criterion | What was evaluated |
|---|---|
| Epistemic fit | Whether the practitioner’s work repeatedly demonstrates premise testing, evidence–inference separation, uncertainty handling, alternative generation, boundary-setting, and revision under counterevidence. |
| Authorship confidence | Whether enough material can be confidently attributed to the practitioner rather than inferred from heavily coauthored work, editorial mediation, or unattributed institutional prose. |
| Corpus accessibility | Whether at least six full-text works can be read legally from an author, institution, repository, publisher open-access page, or other rights-respecting host. |
| Genre and topic diversity | Whether the package extends beyond one homogeneous run of journal articles, ideally including criticism, replies, testimony, lectures, handbooks, essays, or informal working prose. |
| Stylistic distinctiveness | Whether recurring vocabulary, syntax, rhythm, claim posture, and discourse sequencing appear extractable rather than generic to academic prose. |
| Semantic-contamination risk | Whether a feature that appears stylistic is actually a domain instruction or reasoning rule that belongs in `structure.yaml`. Lower risk is better for causal isolation. |

The assessments are ordinal rather than psychometric scores. “Excellent,” “strong,” and similar labels summarize source review; they do not imply validated measurement.

### Access terminology

- **Public full text** means a complete work is legally readable at a public author, institutional, repository, publisher, or government URL.
- **Openly licensed** means the page or file states a reuse licence such as CC BY.
- Public readability does **not** automatically authorize redistribution, bulk storage, model training, or inclusion in a shared research bundle. Before downloading the final corpus, record the licence or permission basis for every item.

All six packages pass the public-full-text threshold provisionally. Ioannidis has the strongest concentration of explicit open licences through PubMed Central. Several Leveson resources state permissions directly. The Gelman, Shaw, Pearl, and Mayo packages contain many lawful author or institutional copies, but item-level reuse licences are heterogeneous or sometimes unstated.

## 1. Nancy Leveson: system-safety review and causal boundary analysis

### Why the package qualifies

Leveson is a recognized system- and software-safety researcher at MIT. Her [MIT faculty profile](https://aeroastro.mit.edu/people/nancy-leveson/) documents her work in system safety and her election to the National Academy of Engineering. The MIT Partnership for Systems Approaches to Safety and Security maintains a centralized [publications repository](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/publications/), a [books and handbooks collection](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/books-and-handbooks/), and a [white-papers collection](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/nancys-white-papers/).

The package comfortably exceeds six full-text works and spans multiple genres:

- an open-access scholarly monograph, [*Engineering a Safer World*](https://direct.mit.edu/books/oa-monograph/2908/Engineering-a-Safer-WorldSystems-Thinking-Applied);
- the public [STPA Handbook](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/get_file.php?name=STPA_handbook.pdf), which states a non-commercial copying and distribution permission;
- multiple solo white papers on safety assurance, risk matrices, Safety-III, and inadvertent nuclear war;
- peer-reviewed papers and technical reports in the PSASS repository;
- public testimony and industry analysis, including [risk management in the oil and gas industry](https://energy.mit.edu/news/risk-management-in-the-oil-and-gas-industry/);
- workshop presentations and teaching material in the [MIT STAMP workshop archive](https://psas.scripts.mit.edu/home/mit-stamp-workshop-presentations/).

### Register hypothesis

The likely register is direct, contrastive, and constraint-oriented. It repeatedly widens the unit of analysis, distinguishes component failure from system-level causal structure, tests whether stated controls are actually adequate, and uses examples to expose limits in conventional assumptions. That is unusually close to the desired behavior of an independent reviewer: inspect the frame, identify missing control relationships, and resist explanations that are locally plausible but systemically incomplete.

### Strengths and risks

- **Epistemic fit:** Excellent. Boundary analysis, causal adequacy, counterexample use, and challenge to received framings are central.
- **Authorship confidence:** Excellent. The corpus contains a solo monograph, solo white papers, testimony, and clearly attributed presentations in addition to coauthored work.
- **Accessibility:** Excellent. Most of the useful material is centralized on MIT or the author’s MIT-hosted project site.
- **Diversity:** Excellent across aviation, nuclear systems, oil and gas, medical devices, software, and organizational safety; genres range from handbooks to testimony.
- **Distinctiveness:** Excellent. The contrastive cadence and system-boundary vocabulary should be detectably different from neutral instructional prose.
- **Semantic-contamination risk:** Very high. “Expand the system boundary,” “model control structures,” and “treat safety as an emergent property” are substantive procedures, not merely a way of wording an invariant procedure.

### Pilot disposition

Advance to extraction-feasibility testing, with aggressive feature classification. A high exclusion rate would itself be informative: it may show that the useful “Leveson register” is substantially constituted by semantic method rather than linguistic realization.

## 2. Andrew Gelman: model criticism and iterative evidence checking

### Why the package qualifies

Gelman is a Columbia statistician whose [official profile](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/) lists major awards in statistical application and methodology. His site provides a very large [published-research archive with direct PDFs](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/published/), while *Statistical Modeling, Causal Inference, and Social Science* supplies a long-running [public working blog](https://statmodeling.stat.columbia.edu/).

The package greatly exceeds six full-text works and offers the strongest genre variation in the candidate pool:

- technical journal articles and conference papers;
- methodological reviews and workflow papers;
- published discussions, rejoinders, and letters;
- magazine essays and public-facing explanations;
- author-filtered blog criticism, corrections, and responses;
- books, book chapters, interviews, and recorded talks linked from the official site.

### Register hypothesis

The likely register is conversational but analytically demanding: start from a concrete example, inspect the model or measurement process, check implications against reality, and revise rather than defend a brittle abstraction. It often separates a useful idea from overclaiming around it. Compared with Leveson, the rhythm is less architectural and more iterative; compared with Mayo, it is generally less prosecutorial.

### Strengths and risks

- **Epistemic fit:** Excellent. Model checking, replication, uncertainty, measurement, and correction are recurring practices.
- **Authorship confidence:** Strong. Solo essays and author-filtered posts can anchor the profile, but many research works are coauthored and the blog is multi-author unless filtered carefully.
- **Accessibility:** Excellent. Public author copies and blog text are abundant and centrally indexed.
- **Diversity:** Excellent in both genre and topic, including statistics, social science, public health, political science, and scientific workflow.
- **Distinctiveness:** Excellent. Short argumentative turns, concrete examples, informal transitions, questions, and occasional humor create a clear signal.
- **Semantic-contamination risk:** High. “Check the model,” “use prior and posterior predictive checks,” and related moves are methodological content. Humor, political examples, and personal disputes could also create irrelevant activation.

### Pilot disposition

Advance to extraction-feasibility testing. Build the candidate package with explicit genre strata and exclude posts selected mainly for personality, politics, or interpersonal controversy. Author-filter blog material rather than treating the blog as a single-author corpus.

## 3. Mary Shaw: architectural comparison and engineering judgment

### Why the package qualifies

Shaw is a pioneering software-architecture researcher and Alan J. Perlis University Professor Emerita at Carnegie Mellon. Her [CMU faculty biography](https://se-phd.s3d.cmu.edu/People/faculty%20and%20staff/faculty-bios/shaw-mary.html) documents her role in establishing software architecture as a research field and her receipt of the U.S. National Medal of Technology and Innovation.

At least six full texts are publicly available from CMU pages and repositories. Representative access points—not final selections—include:

- [“What Makes Good Research in Software Engineering?”](https://www-2.cs.cmu.edu/~Compose/ftp/shaw-fin-etaps.pdf);
- [“An Introduction to Software Architecture”](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/An_Introduction_to_Software_Architecture/6621062);
- [“Some Patterns for Software Architecture”](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Some_Patterns_for_Software_Architecture/6625856);
- [“Patterns for Software Architectures”](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Patterns_for_Software_Architectures/6625199);
- [“Comparing Architectural Design Styles”](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Comparing_architectural_design_styles/6621410);
- [“Architectural Issues in Software Reuse”](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Architectural_Issues_in_Software_Reuse_It_s_Not_Just_the_Functionality_It_s_the_Packaging/6621137);
- [“Formulations and Formalisms in Software Architecture”](https://kilthub.cmu.edu/articles/journal_contribution/Formulations_and_Formalisms_in_Software_Architecture/6605843).

The broader record includes journal and conference papers, essays, position papers, keynotes, historical syntheses, educational writing, and technical reports.

### Register hypothesis

The likely register is taxonomic, comparative, and tradeoff-aware. It identifies the level of abstraction, names alternative formulations, separates functional behavior from packaging or architectural constraints, and evaluates what each representation makes easier or harder to see. For a software or systems reviewer, this may transfer more naturally than a statistics-derived register.

### Strengths and risks

- **Epistemic fit:** Excellent for architecture, design, and research-quality review; less directly evidenced for pressure resistance in conversational settings.
- **Authorship confidence:** Strong. Several useful works are solo-authored, while landmark architecture texts include coauthors.
- **Accessibility:** Strong. CMU hosts public full text, but many KiltHub records are marked “In Copyright”; public reading is clear, downstream redistribution rights require item-level checking.
- **Diversity:** Strong in genre, moderate in topic. The corpus ranges across architecture, reuse, research methods, education, and field history but remains concentrated in software engineering.
- **Distinctiveness:** Strong. Comparative classifications and carefully scoped claims are recurring, though the surface rhythm may be less conspicuous than Gelman’s or Mayo’s.
- **Semantic-contamination risk:** High. Architectural styles, quality criteria, and abstraction-level checks are substantive review operations.

### Pilot disposition

Advance if the pilot role is software architecture, technical design, or research review. Include at least one essay or keynote and one research-methods work so the profile does not collapse into architecture terminology.

## 4. Judea Pearl: causal assumptions and counterexample-driven critique

### Why the package qualifies

Pearl received the ACM A.M. Turing Award for foundational contributions to probabilistic and causal reasoning, as documented by the [ACM award citation](https://amturing.acm.org/award_winners/pearl_2658896.cfm). UCLA hosts a large official [Causal Science Laboratory papers and working-papers archive](https://bayes.cs.ucla.edu/csl_papers.html) with direct full-text links, and the lab maintains a public [causality blog](https://causality.cs.ucla.edu/blog/).

The corpus contains far more than six full texts across:

- formal papers and technical reports;
- short methodological notes;
- published comments, responses, and rejoinders;
- tutorials and introductions;
- keynote and autobiographical essays;
- blog essays and public Q&A, including an [ACM interview](https://cacm.acm.org/acm-turing-award/qa-a-sure-thing/).

A public full-text introduction to causal inference is also available through [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2836213/).

### Register hypothesis

The likely register makes hidden assumptions explicit, distinguishes observational from interventional questions, and uses compact examples or paradoxes to show why an apparently adequate method cannot answer the stated question. It is especially good at forcing the reviewer to ask what causal query is actually being posed.

### Strengths and risks

- **Epistemic fit:** Strong. Assumption exposure, counterexamples, formal distinctions, and resistance to evidentiary overreach are central.
- **Authorship confidence:** Excellent. The UCLA archive contains many solo-authored papers, notes, and commentaries.
- **Accessibility:** Excellent for public reading; many works are direct author-hosted PDFs, though licences are heterogeneous.
- **Diversity:** Strong across technical, expository, historical, argumentative, and conversational genres. Topically, it remains centered on causal inference and AI.
- **Distinctiveness:** Excellent. Rhetorical questions, sharp categorical distinctions, small causal examples, and occasional polemic create a strong signal.
- **Semantic-contamination risk:** Very high. The most salient distinctions—association versus intervention, identification, counterfactuals—are domain semantics. A polemical register may also increase unjustified resistance to correction.

### Pilot disposition

Keep as a strong second-wave candidate. Use only if the semantic reviewer is capable of identifying causal-method content disguised as discourse form, and include nontechnical genres to prevent symbolic notation from dominating the profile.

## 5. John P. A. Ioannidis: evidence reliability and research-system critique

### Why the package qualifies

Ioannidis directs the Meta-Research Innovation Center at Stanford; its [official profile](https://metrics.stanford.edu/people/john-ioannidis) describes his work on bias, replication, and research reliability. The corpus is unusually accessible through PubMed Central and easily clears the six-work threshold.

Representative public full texts, listed only as evidence of feasibility, include:

- [“Why Most Published Research Findings Are False”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1182327/);
- [“How to Make More Published Research True”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4204808/);
- [“Meta-research: Evaluation and Improvement of Research Methods and Practices”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4592065/);
- [“Why Most Clinical Research Is Not Useful”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4915619/);
- [“The Mass Production of Redundant, Misleading, and Conflicted Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5020151/);
- [“Transparency, Bias, and Reproducibility Across Science”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11563668/);
- [“Evolution and Translation of Research Findings”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1851723/).

Available genres include empirical studies, mathematical arguments, essays, manifestos, reviews, invited addresses, editorials, and interviews.

### Register hypothesis

The likely register begins with a strong general claim, decomposes the mechanisms that can make evidence unreliable, quantifies or exemplifies each mechanism, and then shifts outward to incentives and system design. This supports an evaluator who asks not merely “is there evidence?” but “what process generated it, what biases survived, and how likely is replication?”

### Strengths and risks

- **Epistemic fit:** Excellent for evidence quality, publication bias, replication, and systemic incentives.
- **Authorship confidence:** Moderate–strong. Several signature pieces are solo, but much of the broader corpus is heavily coauthored.
- **Accessibility:** Excellent. PubMed Central gives stable full text and item-level licence information; several relevant works are explicitly Creative Commons licensed.
- **Diversity:** Excellent across domains and publication genres, though meta-research concepts recur throughout.
- **Distinctiveness:** Strong. Bold titles and theses, mechanism enumeration, quantitative support, and system-level conclusions recur.
- **Semantic-contamination risk:** High. Bias taxonomies, power, replication, and research incentives are substantive analytical content. The claim posture could also train reflexive contrarianism if extracted crudely.

### Pilot disposition

Advance when rights clarity is prioritized or the target role primarily reviews empirical claims. Balance famous thesis-driven essays with more measured empirical and reflective pieces; otherwise the profile may overrepresent categorical challenge.

## 6. Deborah G. Mayo: severe testing and error-probing argument

### Why the package qualifies

Mayo is a philosopher of statistics whose book *Error and the Growth of Experimental Knowledge* received the Lakatos Award. Her official [publications page](https://errorstatistics.com/mayo-publications/) links papers, chapters, books, exchanges, and her curriculum vitae, while the *Error Statistics Philosophy* site supplies long-form public responses and discussions.

The package clears six full texts across several genres. Public examples include:

- [“Novel Evidence and Severe Tests”](https://errorstatistics.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/mayo-1991_novel-evid-severe-tests.pdf);
- [“Learning from Error”](https://errorstatistics.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/learning-from-error-henle.pdf);
- [“Learning from Error, Severe Testing, and the Growth of Theoretical Knowledge”](https://errorstatistics.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/02/mayo_2010_ei_chapter-1_opt.pdf);
- [a reply to a review of *Statistical Inference as Severe Testing*](https://errorstatistics.com/2020/03/01/replying-to-a-review-of-statistical-inference-as-severe-testing-by-p-bandyopadhyay/);
- [a response to Ben Recht](https://errorstatistics.com/2024/10/22/response-to-ben-rechts-post-what-is-statistics-purpose-on-my-neyman-seminar/);
- [an exchange with Andrew Gelman](https://errorstatistics.com/2024/09/11/an-exchange-between-a-gelman-and-d-mayo-on-abandoning-statistical-significance-5-years-ago/);
- [an external interview](https://blog.apaonline.org/2019/03/07/interview-with-deborah-mayo/).

### Register hypothesis

The likely register is dialectical and prosecutorial. It asks what error a procedure was capable of detecting, whether the evidence passed a test it could readily have failed, and whether a critic’s reformulation actually addresses the original claim. Exchanges and replies reveal behavior under disagreement more directly than polished papers do.

### Strengths and risks

- **Epistemic fit:** Excellent. Error probing, evidential warrant, criticism, and responses to criticism are the substance of the practice.
- **Authorship confidence:** Excellent. Solo papers, chapters, blog essays, and named exchanges are abundant.
- **Accessibility:** Strong. Many works are publicly hosted by the author; reuse licences are often less explicit than in PubMed Central.
- **Diversity:** Moderate. Genre diversity is strong, but topics cluster tightly around philosophy of statistics and evidence.
- **Distinctiveness:** Excellent. Questions, quotations, numbered objections, parenthetical qualifications, and adversarial turns are conspicuous.
- **Semantic-contamination risk:** Very high. “Severity,” “error probing,” and “test the claim in a way likely to reveal its flaws” are direct epistemic instructions. Removing them may leave an argumentative shell with little useful practice signal.

### Pilot disposition

Keep as a high-value stress case rather than the first register. It is particularly useful for testing whether the profile extractor can distinguish linguistic realization from epistemic method. A `stop_register_not_separable` outcome would be plausible and scientifically informative.

## Reserve candidate: David Parnas

Parnas is a compelling intellectual fit for software-design review, modularity, documentation, and professional responsibility. His [McMaster profile](https://www.eng.mcmaster.ca/cas/faculty/prof-david-parnas/) documents his standing and career. Public copies of important papers exist—for example, [“Software Aging”](https://www.cs.kent.edu/~jmaletic/cs63902/Papers/Parnas94.pdf)—but the usable full-text corpus is scattered among publisher pages, personal mirrors, course sites, and repositories, with uneven rights metadata.

He is therefore a reserve, not because of weak epistemic fit, but because acquisition and licence auditing would demand substantially more manual effort than the six ranked packages. Reconsider him if the eventual role is specifically software architecture and professional engineering judgment.

## Cross-candidate interpretation

### Which packages best test the experiment rather than merely improve the agent?

- **Gelman** gives the clearest chance of a strong surface manipulation because formal papers and informal posts express related habits with markedly different rhythm.
- **Leveson** gives the strongest match to independent system review, but also the clearest danger that “style” is inseparable from method.
- **Shaw** is the best role-matched comparator for architecture/design review and may provide a subtler, less theatrical register.
- **Pearl** and **Mayo** are useful hard cases for the semantic-isolation validator because their distinctive discourse moves are tightly coupled to formal epistemic doctrines.
- **Ioannidis** offers the cleanest rights and access path, but package construction must avoid equating independent review with automatic contrarianism.

### Authorship strategy

Do not assume every work in a named practitioner’s orbit reflects that person’s stable register. For the eventual corpus:

1. Use solo-authored work as the stylistic anchor.
2. Admit coauthored work only when the candidate is lead/corresponding author or when style consistency can be demonstrated against the solo anchor.
3. Treat interviews as interviewer-mediated evidence and transcripts as speech, not written prose.
4. Treat edited blog platforms as multi-author unless the page is author-filtered and individually attributed.
5. Record genre, coauthors, venue, revision status, and licence for every item.

### Corpus-composition strategy

A six-to-twelve-work package should not be six adjacent journal papers. A defensible later selection would normally include:

- 2–4 polished research or synthesis works;
- 1–2 critiques, reviews, or comments;
- 1–2 replies, rebuttals, or exchanges;
- 1–2 methodological or process explanations;
- 1–2 less formal pieces such as testimony, lecture text, interview, or author-attributed blog essay.

This is a composition template, not a selection of final works. The final proportions should be token-balanced so one long book or handbook cannot dominate extraction.

## Recommended next decision

Before selecting individual works, bind the pilot to one concrete review role and test **profile separability** on small samples from Leveson, Gelman, and Shaw. For each candidate, extract a provisional feature list from one solo formal work and one solo or clearly attributed informal work, then classify every feature as `realization_only`, `semantic_duplicate`, `semantic_addition`, or `uncertain`.

Compare:

- retention rate after semantic filtering;
- cross-genre recurrence of retained features;
- condition-classification accuracy in a small manipulation check;
- token cost and licence clarity;
- whether the retained profile remains recognizable without names, domain nouns, named methods, or quotations.

Only after this gate should the project select the final six-to-twelve works. A candidate whose distinctive features mostly disappear under semantic filtering is not necessarily a failed expert corpus; it is evidence that the practitioner’s useful behavior is carried more by explicit method than by linguistic register.

## Bottom line

There is no need to begin with paywalled scholarly databases or mass paper downloading. The six ranked packages already provide ample legally readable material from authoritative public sources. The hard problem is not finding six works; it is constructing a genre-balanced, authorship-confident, rights-documented corpus whose retained profile remains stylistically distinctive after semantic content is removed.
