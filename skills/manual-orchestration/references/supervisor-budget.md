# Supervisor scope and budget

Scope: manual-orchestration role guidance; authority remains with the active assignment and owning contracts.
Load when: establishing or revising an objective budget, considering additional scope, or checking capacity before commitments.

## Scope and budget

At the start, record the accepted objective as a short list of original items, and a budget: a dollar limit, any further cost limits or marks, and how many additions trigger reconsideration. Use the user's limits. If they gave none, propose limits in your first report and record them as yours; they apply until the user changes them. The budget does not limit time: waiting for the user costs nothing, but a wall-clock limit kept running through those waits, and its expiry forced a stop and a repeated setup that saved no spend. `check` reports elapsed and model-busy hours as information. `scripts/scope_budget.py` keeps this in the assignment record:
- `init --pool --max-usd` records the objective's dollar limit, and `init --session --provider` binds your own verified session as the objective's root;
- `bind` adds a session no lineage source links, such as a top-level reviewer started outside `role_host.py`; a bound Pi session's pi-subagents children join through their session files without their own binding;
- `cost-limit` sets a further limit or marks for a quota pool (`codex`, `claude_all_models`, which includes Fable, or `claude_fable`) or a dollar pool (`pi_api` for Pi API-key calls, `claude_extra_usage` for direct Pi `anthropic` calls on OAuth), optionally for one session. Direct Pi `anthropic` calls without conclusive auth provenance count against both dollar caps; do not sum the overlapping pools. Direct Pi `openai` calls with an API-key snapshot or inconclusive provenance count toward `pi_api` (the latter as possible API spend); ChatGPT OAuth is not assigned to a dollar or quota pool.

Spend is measured per objective from transcripts by `metrics/scripts/usage_ledger.py tree`. Existing dollar limits use **API-equivalent dollars adjusted for subscription speed**, a cost proxy: the legacy `quota_usd` fields retain their values and units. API-equivalent dollars without that adjustment are reported separately. Neither figure is measured subscription quota. Keep existing grants in their declared units; a revised calibration does not reprice their history or change a user's limit.

The account's weekly meter moves with every running agent, so its rise is never one objective's spend. Account capacity and objective cost are separate constraints. Before committing new work, consider the objective's dollar allowance and account headroom. Allow for the cost of outstanding work, review, repair and closure: spend recorded so far is not a forecast of those costs. A chosen account-wide ceiling is recorded with `init` or `revise --max-weekly-percent`; do not invent a ceiling or infer one from dollars. `check --rollout` discloses the reading's timestamp, reset and freshness; listener measurement views require adoption of the updated reader digest. Stale, missing or reset-unknown readings leave current headroom unknown; the freshness threshold is not a charge-settlement guarantee. Obtain adequate current capacity evidence or reduce the commitment when that uncertainty matters. Experimental objective quota estimates remain null until calibrated; no per-model quota multiplier is inferred from API prices.

Establish owned budget observation before dispatching scope. The bounded manual host can supply foreground observations; `listen` supplies a detached listener when that mechanism is selected. A running listener observes revised limits, and `check` exposes its exit so its owner can restore observation. Explicit `close` records acceptance or abandonment and ends that listener; it does not establish cost settlement. Closed-window totals remain fixed in scope while `tree` and `control` expose current account evidence and separate later bound-session cost candidates. Those candidates require attribution when sessions are reused, and absent candidates do not establish a settled zero tail. Start a new objective with its own record and `init`. With no collected observation route, run `check` before dispatching new scope; it reads spend against the dollar limits from the same ledger. Budget events reach the supervisor; pass down what a child needs rather than creating duplicate spend watchers.

An addition is something the original items do not cover: a new deliverable, component, evidence class or obligation. Fixing a defect in work an original item already covers is not one. Record each addition when you accept it, with its reason. Judge scope by the cumulative change from the original items, not by whether the newest addition looks small. In the S10 session of 2026-10-28/29 (n=1), a plan of three items grew, one gap at a time, into a candidate that also covered bootstrap evidence, consumer validation and controller resource identity; about 14 gaps each went through its own amendment and review cycle, and no real-host run happened in 17.6 hours.

- When additions reach the trigger, classify the cumulative change under [organization formation](organization-formation.md#revision-decisions-and-authority); a large change goes to the user before more dispatch.
- When a user cost limit or a chosen account-wide ceiling is reached, stop: dispatch nothing new, collect outstanding results or handoffs, and report the original items, what is done, each addition and why, what was spent, and the decision needed. A user limit is the user's boundary; do not raise it yourself. Ceiling checks use the last recorded reading in the current account window: a stale reading already above the ceiling retains the stop until a fresh observation resolves it, but a pre-reset or invalid reading cannot establish a current crossing. These window-validity checks apply to `check` and updated readers; older pinned listeners do not supply them. Confirm their ceiling notifications against current-window evidence (for example `check --rollout`) before treating them as current crossings. Unknown headroom itself is an observation requiring judgment, not an automatic grant or a new account-wide stop. A cost mark you set is information for your next decision, not a stop.
