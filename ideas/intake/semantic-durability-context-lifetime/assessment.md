## Claim-level interpretation

### Candidate 1: semantic durability and context-lifetime judgment

The first independently decidable consequence is a model-facing semantic
contract for treating context as conditionally durable working memory. It
combines four useful distinctions:

- preserve meaning rather than transcripts;
- externalize opportunistically when required lifetime, consequence of loss,
  reconstructability, fidelity, and lifecycle cost justify it;
- keep already binding role-state publication obligations stronger than
  economic preference; and
- consider retirement only when continuation is semantically safe and retaining
  the active context is expected to cost more than preserving residual meaning
  and rehydrating a useful working set.

This consequence is not owned by the approved context-decision-observability
proposal. That proposal owns an evidence projection and explicitly leaves the
situated judgment to the authorized role or agent. Wind Walker is the probable
doctrine owner or runtime projection consumer, but formation must decide
whether the full semantic contract belongs canonically in Wind Walker, in
governing design with a compact Wind Walker projection, or in a neighboring
role-profile contract.

The candidate must not treat the hard context limit as semantic safety. It is a
capacity constraint and planning input. Semantic safety remains continuation
independence; effect authority remains separately verified. Any conceptual cost
formula remains a reasoning aid, not a calibrated score, threshold, or reset
instruction.
