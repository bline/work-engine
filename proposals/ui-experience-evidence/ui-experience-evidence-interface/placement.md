# Placement: UI Experience Evidence Interface

## Current candidate

A domain-specific UI experience-evidence façade is the probable semantic
boundary. It composes generic evidence lineage, Chrome observation, product
adapters, provider-analysis producers, accessibility probes, repository
evidence, and UI-review consumers without absorbing their authority.

Permanent physical placement remains uncertain. Formation evidence establishes
a distinct semantic problem but does not yet show whether the façade belongs in
a shared evidence package, an experience-observation capability, a service, a
product adapter framework, or a review-facing projection. Storage and query
acceleration are separate placement questions.

## Candidate owners and seams

### Shared evidence-lineage boundary

Candidate owner of generic stable identity, immutable revisions, provenance,
impact nomination, refresh judgment, exact reliance, and lineage. UI experience
evidence should consume or profile those consequences rather than fork them.
The parent claim-centered proposal remains placement-uncertain, so this is a
hypothesis rather than an accepted dependency owner.

### UI experience-evidence façade

Candidate owner of semantic view contracts, navigation-witness representation,
experience-state and transition topology, evidence-kind correspondence,
accessibility-probe coverage, derived visual-analysis identity, and UI-specific
queries. It does not own acquisition mechanisms or review conclusions.

### Chrome Vision

Generic evidence producer and interaction transport. It owns target/session/
document epochs, bounded operations, artifacts, limitations, and provenance.
It should not acquire product view meaning, navigation safety, claim judgment,
or review authority merely because it produced the evidence.

### Product adapter

Owner of application-specific target meaning, data and actor fixtures, semantic
anchors, view predicates, valid navigation witnesses, state recovery, permitted
mutation, and cleanup. A generic interface cannot safely infer those contracts
from selectors or screenshots alone.

### Accessibility and provider-analysis producers

Evidence producers with specialized mechanisms and limitations. They may emit
probe results or derived observations under explicit contracts. They do not
define the intended access surface, establish accepted claims, or decide UI
quality.

### UI-review roles and coordination

Consumers that interpret evidence through attributed design lenses and publish
review findings. Review artifacts own those findings; coordination owns
selection and synthesis if accepted. Neither should own capture history or
provider cache identity merely because it requested evidence.

### Query index, MCP surface, and control-plane client

Replaceable projections and transports. They may expose current evidence,
coverage, claims, trace topology, history, and controls while retaining source
revision and limitation metadata. They do not become canonical semantic owners
through convenience, persistence, or visual replay.

## Placement risk

Placement risk is high because ownership, state, authority, data, and lifecycle
cross several existing or proposed boundaries. A placement-seams review should
challenge at least:

- view identity versus product-specific state ownership;
- observation applicability versus claim freshness;
- provider cache identity versus evidence authority;
- reviewer evidence requests versus capture mutation authority;
- accessibility applicability versus probe execution;
- canonical history versus query and replay projections; and
- impact nomination versus semantic refresh and downstream reopening.

## Reopening conditions

Reconsider placement when the first real view exercise establishes which fields
and lifetimes are genuinely shared; when a second product exposes incompatible
view or fixture semantics; when provider or accessibility evidence requires a
distinct canonical history; or when concurrency, privacy, retention, delivery,
or recovery requirements force a service boundary. Do not allow the first
working package or store to settle semantic ownership by inertia.
