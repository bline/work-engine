# Future Research Question and Evidence Inventory

## Purpose

This inventory preserves the question families that motivated the proposal
family. It is not an environmental-scoring schema or an implementation design.
Future classifiers and metrics should be recomputable from retained raw
evidence rather than made permanent by today's interpretations.

## Question families

Retained evidence should support later investigation of:

1. exact environment reconstruction at run and observable decision boundaries;
2. declared, exposed, discovered, invoked, forbidden, and unreachable capabilities;
3. procedural prescription, sequencing, gates, exceptions, and accumulated composition;
4. technically permitted, encouraged, constrained, and exercised judgment;
5. ownership, authority, independence, override, disagreement, and deference;
6. evidence existence, visibility, consultation, ordering, conflict, rejection, and later invalidation;
7. context size, composition, instruction competition, and transformation;
8. capability and evidence competition, distance, repetition, and salience proxies;
9. timestamps, event rhythm, latency, density, transitions, and decision pressure;
10. observable challenges, revisions, verification, uncertainty, correction, retries, escalation, and premature closure;
11. expert-practice resemblance distinguished from expert vocabulary or presentation;
12. correctness, tests, findings, defects, rework, regressions, recovery, and later validity;
13. tokens, calls, subagents, retries, wall time, cost, and lifecycle resource use;
14. behavioral changes after pressure, failure, correction, compaction, role transition, or context growth;
15. environment-behavior relationships across sufficiently comparable historical runs;
16. declared and observed harness identity, version, context management, tools, and subagents;
17. contributions and conflicts among harness, instructions, role, skills, workflow, state, artifacts, tools, reviewers, and external events;
18. divergence between declared and effective environment over time;
19. meaningful comparisons among models, harnesses, roles, skill compositions, authority structures, and evidence environments;
20. readiness to design controlled tests after historical correlations nominate candidate effects;
21. provenance and reproducibility of every environmental fact and derived metric; and
22. unknown future questions that require evidence not anticipated by today's metrics.

## Minimum raw evidence

Future analysis requires, where available:

- exact provider requests or their provider-declared logical equivalents;
- responses, observable messages, tool calls, tool results, and runtime events;
- model, reasoning, harness, provider, thread, turn, and parent/child identities;
- configured capability identity, direct exposure, discovery events, invocation,
  and explicit availability failures;
- compaction, summarization, truncation, and context-replacement evidence;
- exact artifact, repository, proposal, claim, plan, checkpoint, review, and
  outcome revisions visible or relied upon;
- timestamps, token usage, tool cycles, failures, retries, and resource evidence;
- intake source and evidence cutoff, proposal formation lineage, slice lineage,
  later findings, corrections, and reopening;
- rejected and contradictory evidence or integrity-bound references to it; and
- raw-source, transformation, classifier, and metric provenance.

## Evidence classes and limits

Keep these classes distinct:

```text
provider/runtime observation
repository observation
structurally derived evidence
model classification
human or authority decision
agent self-report
later outcome evidence
```

Non-use does not prove inaccessibility. Persistence does not prove truth.
Correlation does not prove causality. Internal chain-of-thought, subjective
salience, and the true internal cause of a model choice are not required raw
artifacts and generally cannot be established directly. Retain model-visible
inputs and observable outputs instead.

## Current sufficiency summary

Proposal packets, Git history, checkpoints, receipts, review artifacts, and
metrics strongly preserve durable meaning and many outcomes. Ordinary provider
sessions make temporal, behavioral, and resource questions derivable when they
survive and can be bound. They do not reliably establish exact inference input,
capability exposure, or full cross-stage lineage.

Future Codex rollout tracing can close much of the model-facing environment gap.
Claude remains confidence-qualified. Both still require Work Engine lineage,
retention, sensitivity, exact-subject, and later-outcome bindings.
