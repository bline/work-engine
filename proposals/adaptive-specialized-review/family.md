# Adaptive Specialized Review

## Family identity

- Family ID: `work-engine.adaptive-specialized-review`
- Origin: [`ideas/adaptive-specialized-review-panels.md`](../../ideas/adaptive-specialized-review-panels.md)
- State: candidate family formed from an idea; not reviewed, evaluated, accepted,
  prioritized, or authorized for implementation
- Decision owner: user or future explicitly authorized portfolio owner

This family separates two independently decidable changes that share an origin:

1. [`revision-bound-review-artifacts`](revision-bound-review-artifacts/proposal.md)
   defines the candidate durability, identity, provenance, applicability, and
   lineage consequences for review evidence.
2. [`adaptive-review-panel-coordination`](adaptive-review-panel-coordination/proposal.md)
   defines the candidate judgment capability that selects relevant specialist
   perspectives and preserves a non-authoritative synthesis.

The second candidate has a causal semantic dependency on the first: adaptive
coordination needs a truthful durable target for revision-bound findings and
their applicability. This relationship does not require separate delivery or
acceptance order, and it does not establish that the proposed artifact contract
or coordinator placement is correct.

## Why these are separate proposals

Durable review-artifact semantics can serve more than one coordination route,
and a future coordinator can change without redefining what makes a finding
identifiable, applicable, or non-authoritative. Conversely, useful adaptive
panel judgment is not mechanically established by creating a schema or storing
review prose. The two changes therefore have distinct acceptance questions and
can be revised, rejected, or superseded independently.

This decomposition deliberately does not decide whether panel selection and
synthesis eventually require separate capabilities. Current evidence has not
shown that they are independently decidable product changes. It also does not
decide whether proposal review or a shared proposal-and-implementation substrate
is the final architectural owner.

## Shared consequence

A fresh evaluator can distinguish the proposed review-evidence contract from
the proposed coordination judgment, identify their causal relationship, and
see the unresolved placement and evidence needs without reconstructing the
formation conversation.

## Authority boundary

These packets are formation artifacts. They do not perform review, establish
that either candidate is correct or valuable, change the roadmap, accept an
architecture, decide a proposal, or authorize implementation. Persistence
preserves the provenance of these candidate claims; it does not increase their
epistemic or decision authority.
