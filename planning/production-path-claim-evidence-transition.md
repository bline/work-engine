## Trigger

The successor doctrine requires every production-path claim to have admissible
recording, independent observation, or both. Constraint can preserve the
protected property but cannot establish that it held.

The prior doctrine did not impose that necessity rule. Existing runtime
contracts may therefore protect a required production-path property through a
constraint while providing no evidence mechanism that establishes the claim.
Those contracts become insufficient under the successor doctrine even though
the constraint itself remains valid.

## Transition objective

Identify and reconcile existing constraint-only production-path claims before a
runtime projection, realization, receipt, or acceptance artifact is represented
as satisfying the successor doctrine.

The audit is bounded to:

- runtime instruction projections derived from `DESIGN.md`;
- realization manifests and capability-grant evidence;
- receipts and schemas relied upon to establish production-path claims; and
- acceptance conditions that consume those claims.

Real-code-path trials determine the affected contract set. This record does not
assume that every constraint protects a production-path claim or that every
such claim requires independent observation. Recording remains sufficient when
the owning consumer requires only attributable actor conduct.

## Transition invariants

- A constraint is not reported as evidence that the constrained property held.
- A claim is not narrowed, replaced, or waived by the actor whose realization
  cannot establish it.
- Actor recording is not relabeled as independent observation.
- An unavailable evidence source yields `unestablished`, not inferred success,
  inferred failure, or zero.
- Existing operator and acceptance-condition authority remains unchanged.
- New runtime projections do not reproduce a known constraint-only claim as a
  sufficient contract.

## Required audit disposition

For each candidate contract, the audit records:

- the required claim, subject, covered state, consumption boundary, consumer,
  and authority source;
- the constraint, if any, that preserves the protected property;
- the recording or independently owned observation that establishes the claim;
- whether the bound realization can supply that evidence; and
- one disposition: sufficient as implemented, not a required production-path
  claim, remediation required, or unestablished and routed to the owning
  consumer.

The audit does not change a claim or decide that work may proceed without it.
Those decisions remain with the owner of the dependent acceptance condition.

## Completion and expiry

The transition completes only when:

1. the bounded contract set has an attributed coverage account;
2. every discovered candidate has one required audit disposition;
3. affected projections, realization evidence, receipts, schemas, and tests are
   reconciled or explicitly remain unestablished with an owning decision route;
4. no admitted runtime contract in scope relies on constraint alone to establish
   a required production-path claim; and
5. the transition owner accepts an exact completion record.

The transition expires when that completion record is accepted, when the
successor doctrine is reverted, or when an authorized replacement transition
supersedes it. Completion updates transition state; it does not remove or weaken
the steady-state doctrine in `DESIGN.md`.
