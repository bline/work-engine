## Quote-policy assessment for the operator

The two current failures are **different decisions**, not seven independent demonstrations of inaccuracy. A2-F has one failed `initiator.other_text` anchor in DOC-3 (`clean up` versus `cleanup`), not a fabricated resolution of the E1 remover question in DOC-1. T1 repeats one unattested owner alias on six entries, a substantive provenance defect if the alias is used for authority, but not itself evidence that the extraction missed who verifies termination. See `runs/calibration/A2-F.M2/2/record.json:1–14` and `T1.M2/2/record.json:1–45`. On the final bytes, five of seven typed outputs passed, including all three review-kind outputs. This is useful calibration evidence, not a reliable population rate.

- **Whole-output `strict`** fits an end-to-end question: *Can this package deliver an entirely source-grounded, machine-acceptable artifact?* It is deterministic, requires no repair, and prevents a fabricated alias from entering authority comparison. It also makes an unrelated local slip erase otherwise assessable items, gives longer outputs more opportunities to fail, and imposes a validity gate not applied to every prose quotation. Its attempted-run rate therefore measures **detection plus format/provenance reliability**, not semantic detection alone. Report both denominators, failure class and affected property.
- **Local exclusion with propagation** fits an item-detection question better: keep fatal shape errors fatal; on a failed quote, mark the dependent field/entry, authority result and any dependent item or fallback **not established**. Do not fuzzy-match, infer a corrected span, or let a failed alias establish a match or mismatch. This preserves unrelated evidence, but requires a frozen dependency rule, including what happens when an entry's primary span fails. Merely switching `QUOTE_POLICY` away from `"strict"` does **not** implement this: `comparator.py:663–675` otherwise continues to evaluate the untrusted values.
- **A prespecified primary and sensitivity analysis** could keep integrity failure as one endpoint and show item detection on unaffected evidence from the **same first-pass outputs** as another. That makes the measurement choice visible without rerunning failed arms, choosing the more favorable denominator, or converting calibration into counted evidence. It costs implementation and human-review effort; the two analyses should not be described as independent confirmation.

The relevant operator choice is **whether one bad claim should disqualify the artifact or only the conclusions that rely on it**. Record that choice, the treatment of dependent fields, and the reporting denominators before freeze. None of the options justifies discarding the old failures, silently repairing counted text, or treating this review as execution approval.
