## Material findings requiring revision

1. **Synthesis completeness has an adverse-finding omission loophole.** The
   proposals require references for findings the coordinator says synthesis
   consumed, but do not require an explicit disposition for every material
   finding or conflict produced by the selected review episodes. Referential
   integrity therefore begins too late. The review profile should expose a
   complete episode-output inventory and require an attributed retained,
   deferred, inapplicable, or omitted disposition without pretending machinery
   can judge the disposition's wisdom.

2. **Speculative future-role authority leaked into the review proposal's
   acceptance boundary.** Unresolved research-maintenance and
   organizational-planning roles are not prerequisites for the protected
   consequence of a review-specific artifact profile. Future domain contracts
   must authorize their own transitions; they must not manufacture approval
   dependencies here.

3. **The causal dependency overstates an unproven owner.** Review artifacts
   causally require bounded statement identity, evidence baselines,
   provenance, authority scope, and truthful lineage. They conditionally
   consume the named shared candidate if it is accepted as the owner. The
   relationship should bind causality to the required semantic consequence or
   state its dependency on the candidate conditionally, preserving another
   valid owner if dogfooding rejects the shared placement hypothesis.

4. **Applicability assessment and downstream reliance are collapsed.** The
   downstream decision owner must own readiness, residual-uncertainty reliance,
   and the authority transition. It need not personally produce every advisory
   applicability assessment. The proposals should distinguish an authorized
   attributed assessment from the decision owner's judgment to rely on it.

5. **Composition is named without fully protecting evidence boundaries.** A
   composition relation should identify constituent statement revisions,
   distinct subject/evidence baselines, and coverage gaps, and must not imply
   that the integrated state was jointly reviewed.
